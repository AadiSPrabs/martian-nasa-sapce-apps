1 / 7

The problem

The first astronaut on Mars needs a survival map, not a tourist map.

An EVA is a life-support calculation. Pick the wrong route and power, radiation dose or slope runs out kilometres from shelter — and most Mars maps only show you where things are.

Groundtruth answers the question that matters on the surface: where do we go, how do we get there, and can we get back alive? From orbit, down to the rover traverse.

2 / 7

Orbit — real NASA data

A real globe, not a picture of one.

  • 3D Mars globe (Cesium, Mars ellipsoid) with a 4K MOLA-relief base, streamed Mars Trek tiles at 232 m/px, and true HiRISE detail over Gale.
  • Search 1,794 named features, 34 regions, 30 quadrangles and 8 landing sites. Click any point to fly in.
  • Lighting follows the real Mars sun — a Mars24 model drives the terminator from the current UTC instant.

Sources: NASA/JPL Mars Trek (Viking MDIM21, MOLA/HRSC, HiRISE, regions, nomenclature, landing sites, traverses) · NASA GISS Mars24 · USGS Astrogeology.

3 / 7

EVA anywhere

Pick any point on Mars. Plan a real traverse.

Press EVA, pick any point, search a feature, or enter coordinates. Groundtruth fetches a local 200 m MOLA/HRSC DEM window, builds a local map and an A* rover network, and generates terrain-grounded objectives.

200 mMOLA/HRSC DEM, anywhere
1 mHiRISE terrain at Gale
25–200 kmRecon range selector

Universal layers — Optical, Terrain, Relief, Slope, Geology — work at Gale and at arbitrary sites. Gale is the 1 m field showcase: real Vera Rubin Ridge / Hematite objectives and sample stops.

4 / 7

The planner — consequences, not lines

Every plan gets checked before it gets driven.

  • Choose an objective, pack equipment, compare Fastest / Safest / Most science rover plans.
  • Power, radiation dose (0.026 mSv/h baseline) and slope are checked per plan — 25° is a hard no-go.
  • Run the traverse leg by leg, sample or abort, and close the EVA with a graded field log.

Loadout matters: pack mass changes rover power, and tools gate what you can sample. Solar-event ×3 and a −35% radiation vest are modelled as scenarios, not a live feed.

5 / 7

It runs — measured, not claimed

Built to load fast and say what grade the data is.

The data grade is always visible: MOLA 200 m · RECON outside Gale, 1 m field data inside it. It plans regional rover routes, not boulder-scale walking routes — and says so.

~2.8 sFirst globe canvas
~1.8 sEVA picker
~7 sOlympus terrain + routes

Measured in a headless production build. Cesium runs render-on-demand; the EVA feature is code-split and loads on demand.

Try it · Sources · Honesty

Open it and drive.

Repo: github.com/AadiSPrabs/martian-nasa-sapce-apps
Run it: pnpm install then pnpm dev (Node 20+, pnpm 11+) — production build is a static site.

Data: NASA/JPL Mars Trek · HiRISE (NASA/JPL/UArizona) · MOLA/HRSC blend · USGS Astrogeology SIM3292 geology · NASA GISS Mars24 · MSL REMS weather · MSL RAD radiation baseline (Hassler et al., Science 2014).
AI disclosure: Groundtruth was built with an AI coding agent; all data sources and limitations are documented in the repo README. The repo also contains the earlier single-file Martian Map, built with Muse and independently verified hands-on by a second AI agent, Instinct.
Honest limits: the globe is a smooth ellipsoid with imagery, not a global 3D terrain mesh; anywhere means reconnaissance at 200 m — it does not resolve boulders outside high-res sites; radiation, rover power and thermal values are planning heuristics, not measured crew telemetry.
